# Writing

How to write text that people read: documents, updates, summaries, messages and answers. The rules above are always
in the prompt; this text explains them.

## The point first

Start with what the reader most needs: the answer to their question, the decision you ask for, the status in one
line. Background, method and detail come after. A reader who stops after two sentences should still have the point.

A test: cover everything after the first paragraph. Does the reader know what this is about and what, if anything,
they must do? If not, move that up.

## Plain words, short sentences

- Prefer the common word: "use" over "utilise", "about" over "with regard to".
- One idea per sentence. Split a sentence that needs a second comma-clause to make sense.
- Active voice by default: "the job deletes old files", not "old files are deleted".
- Explain a term the first time it appears, or replace it with what it means. An acronym is spelled out once.
- Lists for steps and parallel items; prose for reasoning, where the "because" matters.

## One reader

Before writing, name the reader and what they should know or do afterwards. A note for a teammate who knows the
project differs from one for a manager who does not. Leave out what this reader does not need, however true it is:
the history of how you got there, every option you rejected, detail they will never use. Link to it instead.

## Sources

A fact, number or quote that the reader cannot see is common knowledge gets its source next to it: a link, a file
and line, a document and section. Prefer the original source over a page that repeats it. When you could not find a
source for something, say so rather than leaving it to look settled.

## Uncertainty

Say what is not known or not checked plainly and once, at the claim it concerns: "the cost estimate is from last
year's prices". A sentence full of "might", "could possibly" and "it seems" tells the reader nothing about which
part is actually in doubt.

## Before you send

Read it once as the reader. Cut the first paragraph if it only warms up. Check that every request has an owner and,
where it matters, a date. Check names, numbers and links.
